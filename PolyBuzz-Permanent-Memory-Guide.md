# PolyBuzz Permanent Memory — Usage and Optimization Guide

## Introduction

### 1. About this guide

This document presents a practical way to understand and use PolyBuzz's permanent memory for long-term RP.

This is **not official PolyBuzz documentation**.

I do not work for PolyBuzz and do not have access to the internal workings of its memory system. The explanations presented here are therefore based on **observations, testing, and usage experience**.

Some information may be incomplete, may change with platform updates, or may contain errors. When discussing how PolyBuzz works internally, it is therefore important to distinguish between what has been **directly observed in use** and what constitutes a possible explanation of the system's behavior.

The purpose of this guide is not to claim with certainty what technically happens inside PolyBuzz, but rather to explain **how the memory behaves in practice and how to use it more effectively**.

### 2. Who this guide is for

This guide is primarily intended for people who use PolyBuzz for RPs long enough for permanent memory to become important.

It may be particularly useful for people who want to:

- understand what is recorded in permanent memory;
- understand which information can be modified manually and which is recorded automatically;
- limit the loss of important information during a long RP;
- better organize the progression of their scenes;
- identify problems that may appear in memory;
- know what to do when important information has been recorded incorrectly;
- optimize the use of the available memory.

### 3. Using the guide with an AI agent

This document is intentionally fairly dense and contains a large amount of practical information.

It can be copied into ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or another AI agent to request additional assistance.

An AI can, for example:

- explain part of the system in simpler terms;
- turn the information in the guide into a step-by-step procedure;
- analyze a memory-related problem;
- help determine what to do in a specific situation;
- rephrase a procedure to make it easier to apply.

However, an AI should not be considered an official source regarding the internal workings of PolyBuzz.

If an AI proposes a technical explanation that is not present in this guide or cannot be directly observed in the interface, that explanation should be treated as an **interpretation or hypothesis**, rather than as official information about the system.

# 1. Understanding Permanent Memory

## 1.1. The three main tabs

Permanent memory is organized into several sections. Not all information is recorded in the same way, and not all of it can be modified manually.

In particular, the following three main tabs should be distinguished:

### Linked

The "Linked" tab contains several categories of information used to preserve elements of the RP.

These include:

- **Important Events**
- **Agreements and Tasks**
- **Important Elements**
- **Report / Relationships**

These categories do not correspond to exactly the same type of information.

Events are primarily used to preserve important elements of what has happened during the RP.

Important elements may concern objects, techniques, powers, or other particular elements that appeared in the RP.

Relationships are used to preserve information concerning relationships between characters.

The information in these categories is primarily recorded automatically by PolyBuzz.

### Character

The "Character" tab concerns the characters present in the RP.

It may concern the bot itself as well as NPCs encountered during the conversation.

The information recorded for a character may include different characteristics such as:

- name;
- nickname;
- age;
- gender;
- sexual orientation;
- likes;
- other important information;
- appearance;
- personality;
- identity;
- residence.

This information may already be present when the RP begins or may be filled in progressively as the RP develops.

It may also evolve during the conversation when new information about a character appears and is recorded in memory.

However, particular care should be taken with this part of the memory.

Unlike the information concerning one's own character, the information recorded for the bot or NPCs cannot simply be modified manually through the interface.

An error recorded in this section can therefore become much more problematic for the continuation of the RP.

### User

The "User" tab concerns only the character played by the user.

This is the part of permanent memory that can be **directly modified by the user**, unlike the information automatically recorded in the other sections.

The different pieces of information concerning the character may include their identity, appearance, personality, or other characteristics available in this part of the interface.

However, not all information can be modified in the same way.

In particular, the **"other important information"** and **"likes"** categories are filled automatically by PolyBuzz and cannot be manually modified in the same way as the other information accessible in this tab.

This is therefore an important distinction to remember:

> The section concerning one's own character is the part of permanent memory over which the user has the most direct manual control, but some information remains managed automatically by PolyBuzz.

# 2. Understanding the Four "Linked" Categories

## 2.1. Important Events

"Important Events" are used to record events that occur during the RP and that PolyBuzz considers important enough to preserve.

The user does not directly create these events.

PolyBuzz automatically decides that a scene or part of a scene should be recorded as an important event.

When something important happens during a scene — for example, something the characters say, do, decide, or experience — PolyBuzz may decide to record it in this category.

This memory therefore plays an important role in RP continuity.

It allows information about what happened previously to be preserved so that these elements can continue to have a place in the rest of the conversation.

However, one essential constraint must be taken into account: **each recorded event has a limited number of tokens**.

An event therefore cannot continue accumulating new information indefinitely.

At the beginning of a scene, important information may be recorded progressively and in a relatively chronological manner.

But if the same scene continues for a very long time, the corresponding event may eventually reach its limit.

When this happens, new information must continue to fit within the available space.

Some older information may then be considered less important compared with more recent information.

It may be:

- condensed;
- replaced;
- simplified;
- or eventually disappear.

This is one reason why information may have been correctly recorded at the beginning of a scene and then no longer appear in the same way later.

In a long RP, this category therefore requires particular attention.

It is not enough to keep a scene going indefinitely on the assumption that everything that happens within it will automatically remain available.

## 2.2. Agreements and Tasks

The "Agreements and Tasks" category concerns elements corresponding, among other things, to planned actions or commitments made during the RP.

This may include:

- appointments;
- missions;
- tasks to be completed;
- commitments;
- planned actions.

It is useful to distinguish between what has **already been completed** and what still **needs to be done**.

This distinction helps clarify the role of this category in RP continuity.

When an action is planned but has not yet taken place, it should not be treated in the same way as an event that has already been completed.

## 2.3. Important Elements

"Important Elements" concern particular elements that appear during the RP and may be important for what follows.

This may include:

- objects;
- techniques;
- powers;
- abilities;
- or other particular elements that appeared in the RP.

This information is recorded automatically by PolyBuzz.

In general, this category causes fewer problems than some other parts of memory, but it remains important for RPs in which objects, abilities, or particular elements need to be preserved for continuity.

## 2.4. Report / Relationships

The relationship category is used to preserve information about the relationships between characters.

It may concern, for example, the relationship between the bot or an NPC and the character played by the user.

The relationship may evolve throughout the RP, and certain information may then be recorded in this part of the memory.

However, a relationship may sometimes be misunderstood or incorrectly phrased by PolyBuzz.

This is generally not the category that causes the most problems, but it is still useful to check it when an important relationship changes significantly during the RP.

# 3. Properly Anchoring the Beginning of the RP

## 3.1. Why the beginning matters

The first message can provide PolyBuzz with precise reference points for the beginning of the scene.

Character starters do not always contain a sufficiently precise date and location.

The user can therefore provide this information themselves in the first message.

The goal is to provide a sufficiently clear starting point so that the scene immediately has temporal, spatial, and narrative reference points.

A vague beginning provides fewer precise pieces of information to which later events can be connected.

## 3.2. What to anchor

When relevant to the scenario, the first message may specify:

- the date;
- the time;
- the location;
- the situation;
- the people present;
- the initial state of the relationship;
- previously established events;
- particular elements specific to the scenario.

The date, time, and location are particularly useful for establishing a clear reference point from the start.

There is no need to turn every first message into a technical sheet.

The idea is simply to provide the important information needed to understand precisely **where, when, and in what situation the scene begins**.

## 3.3. Concrete example

For example:

> *It is August 16, 2026, 8:32 PM when I arrive at Thomas's house to celebrate his birthday. I smile at him and, when he opens the door, I say:*
>
> "Sorry, I'm late."

This first message immediately establishes several reference points:

- **date:** August 16, 2026;
- **time:** 8:32 PM;
- **location:** Thomas's house;
- **situation:** arriving for his birthday;
- **character present:** Thomas;
- **initial action:** arrival and beginning of the interaction.

This gives PolyBuzz precise information about the starting point of the scene.

After this first message has been sent, permanent memory can be activated.

PolyBuzz can then use the information present in the script, the character's starter, as well as important information introduced in the user's message.

The RP can then progress normally.

## 3.4. Why this helps memory

The first message provides the initial reference points to which subsequent information can be connected.

A precise date, time, and location provide a clearer framework for the scene.

As the RP then develops, new events already have an established starting context.

Conversely, when an RP begins in a very vague situation, it is more difficult to maintain a clearly identifiable chronology.

Anchoring the beginning obviously does not guarantee that PolyBuzz will subsequently record all information correctly.

It simply provides the system with much more precise reference points from the beginning.

# 4. Important Events and Their Limit

## 4.1. Each event has a limited capacity

Each event recorded at a specific date has a limited number of tokens.

This means that an event cannot contain an infinite amount of information.

At first, PolyBuzz may progressively record the important information from the scene as it develops.

But if the user remains in the same scene for a very long time, the corresponding event continues to grow.

A very long scene does not automatically create more memory space inside that event.

When the limit is reached, new information must continue to be integrated into the available space.

PolyBuzz may then consider some older information less important than more recent information.

Older information can therefore be condensed, replaced, or removed.

## 4.2. How information loss can appear

The loss of previously recorded information can appear in several different ways.

Information may be:

- condensed;
- simplified;
- replaced by a more recent formulation;
- stripped of certain details;
- or disappear completely from the event.

This does not necessarily mean that the information had never been recorded.

It may have been present previously and then modified as the event continued accumulating new information.

This is precisely the phenomenon that can become problematic in very long RPs.

A scene that continues for a very large number of exchanges can gradually cause details that previously seemed established to be lost.

## 4.3. Why "playing more" does not necessarily mean "recording more"

It may seem logical to assume that the longer a scene is, the more information it will preserve.

In practice, this is not necessarily the case.

If all information continues being added to the same event, that event remains subject to its token limit.

Keeping a single scene going indefinitely therefore does not necessarily create more memory space.

This is why it is important to recognize when a scene has developed sufficiently and when it becomes preferable to move on to a new stage of the RP.

The question is therefore not simply **how long you play**, but also **how you distribute the progression of the RP across the different events recorded by PolyBuzz**.

# 5. Effectively Separating Events
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## 5.1. Why create new events

"Important Events" are not manually created by the user. PolyBuzz automatically decides that an event should be recorded when it considers a scene important or when something interesting happens within it.

The goal is therefore not to try to create an event yourself through the interface, but rather to **let the RP progress in a sufficiently clear way for PolyBuzz to distinguish between different scenes**.

This separation becomes particularly important when the current scene starts becoming long. Since each event has a limited number of tokens, continuing indefinitely within the same event can lead to older information being gradually condensed, replaced, or disappearing.

It is therefore preferable, when a scene has accomplished what it needed to accomplish, to close it and move on to a new stage of the RP.

The goal is not necessarily to change the story or abandon the current plot. A new scene can perfectly well continue the exact same conversation, relationship, or plot.

The point is simply **not to keep filling the same event indefinitely** once the RP has genuinely moved on to a new scene.

---

## 5.2. Using transitions and time skips

A practical way to move from one scene to another is to use a short transition in the form of a time skip.

The previous scene can be clearly concluded, after which a new scene can begin with new reference points.

An effective transition may include:

- the end of the previous scene;
- a time skip indicating that time has passed;
- a new date;
- optionally, a new time;
- a new location;
- a new situation;
- and then the continuation of the RP.

The time skip makes it possible to avoid playing every detail between two important moments.

It also allows continuity to be preserved: what happened previously can still have consequences for the new scene.

The idea is therefore not to erase what came before, but to **properly close one stage and begin another**.

---

## 5.3. Example of a complete transition

Imagine that the initial scene takes place on August 16, 2026, at Thomas's house, during an important conversation.

After developing this scene sufficiently, one could write:

*We continue talking quietly for the rest of the night, until each of us eventually heads home. I keep our conversation in mind, convinced that it is not over.*

*On August 18, 2026, I meet Thomas at the bar to continue our discussion.*

This transition does several things.

The first scene is clearly finished.

A time skip then moves the RP to the next moment without requiring every passing hour to be played out.

Then a **new date** is given: August 18, 2026.

A **new location** is also given: the bar.

Finally, narrative continuity is preserved because the new scene indicates that the previous conversation will be continued.

This does not start a new story: it simply creates a new reference point for a new stage of the RP.

---

## 5.4. Beware of overly vague wording

A time skip can allow PolyBuzz to create a new event, but this does not mean that any wording will necessarily produce a clear separation.

For example, imagine that a first scene takes place on August 16, 2026.

The scene can be ended with:

*The next day, I meet Thomas at the bar to continue our discussion.*

PolyBuzz may then create a new event from this transition. It has, among other things, a new temporal reference point — "the next day" — as well as a new location: "the bar."

The problem appears when very similar reference points are used again afterward.

For example:

*The next day, I meet Thomas at the bar.*

From our perspective, the situation is perfectly clear: another day has passed and a new scene is beginning.

But PolyBuzz may not make the same distinction.

It once again encounters a similar relative reference — "the next day" — associated with the same location — "the bar." It may therefore consider itself to still be within the same event it has just recorded.

The previous event consequently continues growing even though, from our perspective, several different scenes have already taken place.

Because the event has a token limit, this can eventually cause older information to be progressively lost.

Information that had initially been recorded correctly may then be condensed, replaced, or removed to make room for more recent information.

This is why one should not rely solely on the fact that a new scene is obvious to us.

PolyBuzz should also be given **sufficiently distinct reference points** to encourage this separation.

---

## 5.5. Providing sufficiently distinct reference points

To properly separate two events, temporal and spatial reference points are particularly important.

The content of the scene obviously remains essential to the story, but when the goal is to help PolyBuzz understand that a new stage is beginning, it is preferable to provide information that clearly differentiates the scene from the previous one.

The main reference points to prioritize are:

- **Date**
- **Time**, when relevant
- **Location**
- **Situation**
- **New context**
- **Clear change in the narrative stage**

The most important reference point is, in particular, to provide **a precise date and a precise location** when you genuinely want to start a new event.

For example:

*On August 18, 2026, I meet Thomas at the bar to continue our discussion.*

This wording immediately gives PolyBuzz a precise new temporal reference point as well as a new spatial context.

It is therefore clearer than a succession of relative formulations such as "the next day," especially when those formulations are repeated several times.

This is not an absolute guarantee regarding how PolyBuzz will record the scene, since events are created automatically by the platform. It simply provides much clearer reference points for distinguishing the different stages of the RP.

# 6. Playing to Optimize Memory
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## 6.1. The central principle

The goal is not to try to make every sentence of the RP get recorded.

Permanent memory is not intended to preserve the entire conversation. What matters is that information that will be useful for the continuation of the RP can be retained.

It is therefore preferable to make the elements that genuinely matter for continuity clearly emerge.

This may include:

- an event that changes the situation;
- an important decision;
- new information;
- an evolving relationship;
- an action that will have consequences later;
- an object, ability, or particular element that becomes important.

The idea is therefore to prioritize information that has a genuine function in the continuation of the RP rather than trying to force everything into memory.

---

## 6.2. Playing important events until they stabilize

When an important event occurs, it is preferable to give it enough room for the situation to become properly established.

An item of information may need to be developed before it becomes clear in the continuity of the RP.

For example, an important discussion may lead to a decision and then to a consequence. If the scene is immediately interrupted before that consequence appears, the event may be less clearly defined than if the scene is developed sufficiently.

This does not mean cutting a scene as soon as a new element appears.

Rather, two situations should be distinguished:

- when an important event is still developing, it may be useful to continue playing it;
- when it is sufficiently established and the scene then becomes repetitive or anecdotal, it may be preferable to move on to a new stage.

The goal is for the recorded event to correspond to something sufficiently clear to be useful later.

---

## 6.3. Avoid wasting memory space

Since each event has a limited capacity, it is preferable to avoid unnecessarily prolonging the same scene.

Elements that have no consequences for what follows can occupy space even though they gradually become less important.

This may include:

- purely repetitive discussions;
- small exchanges without consequences;
- descriptions that will have no future use;
- repetitions of information that has already been established;
- passages where the situation is no longer genuinely changing.

This does not mean that every quiet scene or conversation without major stakes should be removed.

The goal is simply to recognize when a scene has stopped adding anything new to the continuity.

If an important scene is still developing, it is perfectly possible to continue it. The problem mainly appears when the same scene continues growing even though its content has become essentially anecdotal.

---

## 6.4. Make consequences appear

When an element is meant to have lasting importance, it is useful for its consequences to appear clearly in the RP.

This may be:

- a decision;
- a promise;
- a new relationship;
- a change in behavior;
- a new object;
- a mission;
- important information;
- a lasting consequence.

Making these consequences appear gives the event more substance.

For example, a decision made during a conversation becomes more important when it subsequently leads to an action or changes the situation between the characters.

Memory should therefore not only preserve what has just been said. It is also important for important elements to produce something in the progression of the RP.

# 7. Managing RP Progression
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## 7.1. Do not stay in the same event for too long

You need to learn to recognize when a scene has fulfilled its purpose.

A scene may begin with an important event, develop for a while, and then reach a point where the situation is sufficiently established.

From that point onward, if the exchanges start going in circles or become mostly anecdotal, it may be preferable to move on to a new stage.

This does not mean abandoning the plot.

A new scene can perfectly well pick up the consequences of the previous one, continue a discussion, or carry on exactly the same plot.

The separation simply serves to avoid continuing to fill the same event indefinitely.

---

## 7.2. Use time skips intelligently

A time skip allows the RP to move forward without playing every intermediate moment.

Everything that happens between two important stages does not necessarily need to be developed in detail.

A period can therefore be summarized when it contains nothing that needs to be preserved precisely.

For example, instead of playing every minute of an evening or every day between two important events, it is possible to summarize that period and resume directly when something important happens.

This has two benefits:

- advancing the RP more efficiently;
- avoiding unnecessarily filling an event with information that will have no importance later.

The time skip should nevertheless be clear enough for the RP to continue without ambiguity.

---

## 7.3. Do not artificially remove continuity

Moving to a new scene does not mean erasing what happened before.

A time skip should not be used to artificially remove the consequences of an event.

If a decision was made, a relationship changed, a mission was assigned, or an event altered the situation, these elements must continue to exist in subsequent scenes.

The new scene should therefore be considered a new stage of the RP, not a new beginning unrelated to what came before.

For example, if two characters had an important discussion in one scene, the next scene can begin several days later while still referring to the consequences of that discussion.

The goal is to separate events in memory without breaking narrative continuity.

# 8. Modifying or Correcting Stored Information
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## 8.1. What can be modified manually

The part of permanent memory that the user can actually modify directly concerns their own character, in the "User" tab.

The information available for this character can be adjusted manually within the categories provided by the interface.

However, an important distinction must be made: "likes" and "other important information" are filled automatically by PolyBuzz and cannot be manually modified in the same way as the other information.

For the other tabs, particularly information concerning the bot, NPCs, and elements linked to the RP, recording is primarily performed automatically by PolyBuzz.

One should therefore not assume that every piece of information visible in memory can be directly edited by the user.

---

## 8.2. Automatic modification by PolyBuzz

A significant portion of memory is populated automatically by PolyBuzz.

This includes information that appears progressively throughout the RP in the different memory categories.

The user can indirectly influence what is recorded by playing and clearly introducing important information, but this does not mean they directly control the recording process.

PolyBuzz itself decides which information should be retained and how it should be phrased.

This may work correctly, but errors can also occur.

These may include:

- an incorrect date;
- a character merge;
- an incorrect association between a date and a location;
- incorrectly phrased information;
- a misunderstood relationship;
- incorrect information attributed to a character;
- or, in some cases, a characteristic that eventually influences a character's behavior in a problematic way.

It is therefore important to regularly check what is actually present in memory.

---

## 8.3. Attempting to modify memory via OOC

It is sometimes possible to attempt to request a memory modification directly from the bot by using an out-of-character message.

You can, for example:

- write a message in parentheses;
- use "OOC";
- explicitly request a modification;
- ask the bot to modify a stored piece of information.

For example, you can explain out of character that a recorded piece of information is incorrect and ask for it to be corrected.

This method can sometimes produce a useful result, but it has important limitations.

### Limitations

An OOC request is not a guaranteed system console.

The bot may:

- understand the request;
- respond that it has made the modification;
- respond as if it had access to the memory;
- or simply treat the message as conversational content.

It is therefore possible for a response to give the impression that the information has been modified when the permanent memory has actually remained unchanged.

The behavior may also depend on how the bot was built and on the instructions present in its script.

This method should therefore be considered a possible attempt, not a reliable method of directly controlling PolyBuzz's memory.

---

## 8.4. When the script can help

The bot's script may nevertheless have a specific mechanism for OOC requests.

This may be the case:

- if the author has explicitly provided an OOC command;
- if the script states that certain formulations should simulate a console or system address;
- if the bot was designed to interpret this type of request as a modification instruction.

In such cases, the bot may be more likely to understand the request as an out-of-character instruction.

However, this does not automatically mean that PolyBuzz's permanent memory will actually be modified.

The behavior specified by the script and the platform's actual memory system remain two different things.

---

## 8.5. Why this method remains unreliable

A model connected to the platform may interpret a request addressed to the "system" as ordinary conversation.

Writing:

"OOC: modify this information in your memory"

does not automatically turn the message into a genuine system instruction.

The bot may simply respond:

"It's fixed."

without the information displayed in permanent memory actually changing.

This is why it is always necessary to distinguish between what the bot claims to have done and what actually appears in the memory interface.

The platform's actual memory remains separate from what the model claims to have modified.

# 9. Checking What Was Actually Recorded
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## 9.1. Do not confuse the bot's response with actual memory

The bot's response should not be considered proof that memory has been modified.

A bot can perfectly well understand a request, confirm a correction, and continue the conversation as if it had been performed.

This does not tell us whether PolyBuzz actually modified the information.

The only reliable way to verify the result is to look directly at permanent memory in the interface.

---

## 9.2. Check the relevant categories

Depending on the type of information being searched for, the corresponding tab should be checked:

- "Linked";
- "Character";
- "User".

In "Linked", check the categories corresponding to events, important elements, agreements and tasks, or relationships.

In "Character", check the information concerning the bot and NPCs.

In "User", check the information concerning the character played by the user.

This verification can also reveal errors that were not necessarily noticed during the RP.

---

## 9.3. Check after a modification

When an item of information is important, a simple three-step verification can be performed:

1. check the information before the modification;
2. make the modification request;
3. check the memory again after the modification.

It is then possible to compare what was present before with what actually appears afterward.

This method is particularly useful when a modification has been requested through OOC.

If the bot claims to have made the modification but the information remains unchanged in memory, the modification should be considered unsuccessful.

# 10. What to Do When Important Information Has Disappeared
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## 10.1. Identify what disappeared

When information appears to have been lost, the first step is to determine precisely what is missing.

It may be:

- character information;
- an event;
- a relationship;
- an object;
- a task;
- a consequence;
- a chronological element.

This distinction is important because not all information is recorded in the same part of memory and cannot all be corrected in the same way.

---

## 10.2. Determine why it may have disappeared

There may be several possible causes for information loss.

In "Important Events," a scene that is too long may have reached its token limit. Older information may then have been condensed, replaced, or removed in favor of more recent information.

Information may also have been recorded incorrectly from the beginning, particularly when it concerns a date, location, or association between several elements.

In the "Character" tab, the problem may be different. Information may have been assigned to the wrong character, two characters may have been merged, or a characteristic may have been recorded in a way that subsequently influences the character's behavior.

The memory itself should therefore be examined before concluding that this is simply a case of information loss.

---

## 10.3. Reintroduce the information into the RP

When important information has disappeared but can still be naturally reintroduced, it is possible to bring it back into the RP.

The idea is not necessarily to mechanically repeat the original sentence.

Instead, you can:

- make the information reappear naturally;
- give it a new context;
- produce a relevant consequence or reminder;
- then check whether PolyBuzz has recorded it again.

For example, if an element important to the continuation no longer appears in memory, it may be useful to bring it back into the RP when it naturally becomes relevant to the story.

The memory should then be checked to verify that the information has actually been recorded.

This method is mainly suited to information that can still be reintroduced without creating a contradiction with what has already happened.

When incorrect information in the "Character" tab instead begins to permanently affect a character's behavior, simply reintroducing the correct information into the RP may not be enough. In that case, it may be necessary to consider a rollback.

# 11. Rollback
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## 11.1. What rollback is for

Rollback allows you to return to an earlier state of the RP when the current progression creates a problem that cannot easily be corrected within the continuity of the conversation.

It can be useful when the RP has gone in the wrong direction, when important information has been recorded incorrectly, or when an evolution of a character or relationship has become problematic.

In the context of permanent memory, rollback is particularly useful because it may allow you to return to an earlier state before certain problematic information was recorded or before an undesirable development became established in memory.

However, this is not simply a matter of returning to a previous message in the conversation.

Rollback should be understood as a way of returning to an earlier state of the RP as it existed at a given point, with the information and progression associated with that state.

Its main purpose is therefore to **undo a problematic development rather than having to try to repair it afterward**.

---

## 11.2. When to use rollback

Rollback may be considered when the problem is significant enough to justify going back.

This may include:

- a major error in the progression of the RP;
- an undesirable evolution of the relationship between characters;
- important information that was recorded incorrectly;
- an event that was misunderstood or incorrectly memorized;
- a characteristic assigned to the wrong character;
- behavioral drift in a character;
- a contradiction that has become difficult to correct naturally;
- a situation that has caused several incorrect pieces of information to enter memory.

Rollback is particularly relevant when an error starts having consequences across multiple scenes.

For example, incorrect information may appear in a character's memory. The bot may then begin behaving according to that information, after which several scenes may be built around the new characteristic.

In such a case, correcting only the latest consequence may not be enough.

Returning to a state before the error appeared may be cleaner than trying to repair every consequence individually.

However, it is preferable not to use rollback for small errors that can be corrected naturally within the RP.

When information can simply be reintroduced or corrected without creating a contradiction, it is generally easier to continue the RP and gradually restore the situation.

---

## 11.3. Limitations

Rollback should not be considered a perfect backup of the entire RP.

One should not assume that returning to an earlier state automatically guarantees that **all** memory information will be exactly identical to what existed previously.

Permanent memory and the progression of the conversation are related, but they should not be considered perfect copies of one another.

After a rollback, it is therefore important to check what was actually restored.

In particular, check:

- "Important Events";
- "Agreements and Tasks";
- "Important Elements";
- "Report";
- character information;
- user character information;
- as well as important chronological elements.

An apparent restoration should therefore not be considered sufficient without verification.

Rollback should instead be viewed as **a means of returning to an earlier situation**, after which memory must be checked and the RP can potentially continue from that state.

It is also preferable not to systematically rely on rollback to repair memory loss.

The best strategy remains to prevent errors and losses by properly structuring the RP from the beginning.

# 12. General Strategy for Long RPs
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## 12.1. Before starting

The best memory optimization begins before the RP even becomes long.

Before starting, it is useful to check the information already present in permanent memory in order to know exactly what state the characters and context are currently in.

In particular, check:

- the scenario's starting information;
- the character played by the user;
- the bot's information;
- important NPCs;
- elements already present in memory;
- any events or relationships already recorded.

This helps prevent starting a new RP with incorrect information already present in memory.

It is also important to properly anchor the beginning of the RP.

The date, location, people present, situation, and initial state of the relationships should be sufficiently clear to provide PolyBuzz with solid reference points.

When the scenario already contains elements established before the scene begins, these should also be sufficiently clear to avoid ambiguity about the starting point.

The goal is not to fill memory with the maximum possible amount of information before starting.

It is rather to **ensure that the information that genuinely forms the basis of the RP is correct and consistent**.

---

## 12.2. During the RP

During the RP, optimization mainly depends on how events are built and separated.

In particular:

- develop important events;
- create clear transitions between different scenes;
- avoid unnecessarily overloading the same event;
- make important consequences appear;
- periodically check memory;
- correct errors when they are identified.

When a scene introduces important information, it is preferable to give it enough context for that information to be clearly established.

Conversely, when the scene has fulfilled its purpose and exchanges have become mostly repetitive, it may be preferable to move on to a new stage.

It is also important to monitor information that evolves progressively.

A relationship may change.

A character may acquire a new important characteristic.

A mission may be completed.

An object may change ownership.

A decision may produce a lasting consequence.

These changes should be clearly identifiable in the RP and, when relevant, checked in memory.

However, monitoring should not become constant.

The goal is not to interrupt the RP after every message to check memory, but to perform checks at appropriate moments, particularly after important events or when critical information has just appeared.

---

## 12.3. When an important event is finished

When an important event reaches its conclusion, it is preferable to clearly recognize that this stage has ended.

This can be done through a natural conclusion to the scene, followed by a transition to what comes next.

A new stage can then begin with new temporal, spatial, or narrative reference points.

For example:

*The discussion finally ends after several hours. We have now made a clear decision regarding the mission, and everyone knows what they will have to do.*

*On August 21, 2026, three days later, we meet at the train station to begin the mission.*

The first scene therefore has an identifiable conclusion.

The new scene then has a different date and location, as well as a new situation: the beginning of the mission.

Continuity has not been removed.

On the contrary, the new scene directly builds on the decision made previously.

This approach makes it possible to treat the RP as a succession of stages rather than a single scene that continues indefinitely.

---

## 12.4. When critical information appears

When particularly important information appears in the RP, it is preferable to give it special attention.

This may include:

- essential information about a character;
- a decision that will change the continuation of the scenario;
- a major change in a relationship;
- an important object;
- a mission;
- a promise;
- a lasting consequence;
- an essential chronological element.

This information should first be clearly established in the RP.

It is then useful to check which memory category it appears in.

For example, a relationship development may be found under "Report," while a task or mission may appear under "Agreements and Tasks."

An important narrative event may appear under "Important Events," while an object or particular element may be recorded under "Important Elements."

When information directly concerns a character, the "Character" or "User" tab should also be checked, depending on which character is involved.

Once the information has been recorded, it may be useful to check it again after several scenes have progressed.

This makes it possible to determine whether the information remains present or whether it has been condensed, modified, or replaced.

For elements that will have a major influence on the continuation of the RP, this monitoring is particularly important.

# 13. Key Takeaways
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

## Principle 1

Permanent memory is not a complete recording of the RP.

It should not be considered a complete transcript of every message or every detail of the conversation.

Its role is instead to preserve certain elements considered important so that the RP can continue coherently.

---

## Principle 2

Not all memory categories serve the same purpose.

"Important Events," "Agreements and Tasks," "Important Elements," and "Report" are used to preserve different types of information.

Likewise, the "Character" and "User" tabs concern information relating to characters.

It is therefore important to know where to look for information when checking whether it has actually been recorded.

---

## Principle 3

"Important Events" have a limited capacity.

An event can continue accumulating information throughout a scene, but its available space is not unlimited.

When too much information accumulates, older elements may gradually be condensed, replaced, or disappear.

An event should therefore not be treated as a permanent memory space capable of containing an entire scene indefinitely.

---

## Principle 4

A long RP should be thought of as a succession of stages rather than one endless scene.

Each important stage can be developed sufficiently to establish it clearly, after which the RP can move to a new scene when appropriate.

This organization helps limit the overloading of a single event while preserving narrative continuity.

---

## Principle 5

Clear transitions help separate events more effectively.

A new date, a new location, a new situation, or a clear change in context can provide reference points that distinguish a new stage from the previous one.

However, this is not an absolute guarantee regarding how PolyBuzz will record events.

These reference points mainly serve to make the progression of the RP more clearly identifiable.

---

## Principle 6

What matters is not preserving every detail, but preserving information that will have consequences for what follows.

A conversation without consequences does not necessarily need to be preserved in every detail.

Conversely, a decision, promise, relationship, mission, or piece of information that will affect later scenes deserves greater attention.

The goal is therefore to prioritize narrative value and continuity rather than the raw quantity of recorded information.

---

## Principle 7

An OOC request can sometimes help request a modification, but it is not a guaranteed command.

Asking the bot to modify information does not necessarily mean that PolyBuzz will actually modify its permanent memory.

A positive response from the bot is therefore not enough to confirm that a modification has been made.

The memory displayed in the interface must be checked directly.

---

## Principle 8

The memory displayed by PolyBuzz should remain the authority on what has actually been recorded.

The bot's behavior may provide an indication, but it is not sufficient proof.

When information is important, the relevant tab should be checked directly to determine what is actually present in memory.

---

## Principle 9

The best optimization consists of working with the actual behavior of memory rather than trying to force everything into it.

There is no need to turn permanent memory into a complete archive of the RP.

It is more effective to:

- structure scenes properly;
- make important events clearly emerge;
- separate RP stages;
- limit unnecessary repetition;
- make consequences appear;
- check critical information;
- correct errors when they are identified;
- use rollback when the situation warrants it.

Memory should therefore be treated as a resource to manage, rather than a space in which the entire RP could be stored without limitation.

---

# Conclusion
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Permanent memory can considerably improve the consistency of a long RP, but it has its own constraints.

It does not function as a complete backup of the entire conversation.

Its effectiveness depends in particular on how important information appears, evolves, and is preserved throughout the RP.

The goal is therefore not to try to record absolutely everything that happens.

Instead, it is about learning to:

- properly structure the beginning of the RP;
- clearly establish important events;
- give enough substance to events that need to matter;
- separate the different stages of the story;
- use transitions and time skips appropriately;
- limit the overloading of a single event;
- make important consequences appear;
- monitor what is actually being remembered;
- correct information when necessary;
- use rollback when an important error can no longer be cleanly corrected;
- and make intelligent use of the available memory space.

The general logic can therefore be summarized simply:

**establish → develop → stabilize → verify → transition → continue.**

This does not mean directly controlling PolyBuzz's memory, since its recording process is largely automatic.

Rather, it means constructing the RP in a way that provides the platform with sufficiently clear reference points, while monitoring what is actually being retained.

This method is based on observations of how PolyBuzz behaves and may require adjustments if the platform changes its memory system.

It should therefore not be considered an absolute guarantee regarding the platform's internal workings, but rather as a practical usage method based on observation and experience.
